Your Mac reads it to you, word by word.

A menu bar app. Press ⌥R and it reads the window in front of you out loud, while a yellow box follows each word.

All the shortcuts ↓

⌥R and it reads.

One shortcut, in any app. It takes the window as you see it, finds the words and reads them in order. The box moves with the voice, so your eyes never lose the line.

Space pauses. Esc stops. ↑ and ↓ change the speed.

Start anywhere.

Hold ⌥ and click a sentence. It starts reading from the beginning of that sentence, not from the top of the page.

← jumps back a sentence, → skips ahead. Hold → to hear it at 2x.

An octopus has eight arms and three hearts. Its blood is blue. Like other cephalopods, it has two eyes and a beaked mouth at the centre point of its arms.

It skips the clutter.

Menus, tabs, buttons and side panels are not read. The first time it meets an app or a website, Claude looks at the screen once and writes a reading plan for it: what is interface and what is content. Next time the plan is already there.

Tell it what to skip.

Hearing something useless? Press ⌫ while it reads. That line goes, and so does everything like it: delete one "For other uses" note on Wikipedia and every note of that kind is skipped from then on.

Changed your mind? ⌥⌫ undoes the last one.

Explained mode.

Class notes are short and full of arrows. When it sees notes, it stops reading them word for word and explains them like a teacher would, in a story that follows on from the last point. The box still moves over your own words.

⌥E explains just the passage you are on. ⌥Q lets you ask a question out loud. Explained mode speaks Spanish for now.

The note

IPC sube → poder adquisitivo baja: mismo sueldo, compra menos.

Prices go up, so what your salary can buy goes down. The note is example data; the sentence is the app's real output.

How it works.

Native Swift, no browser extension. Three parts, each doing one job.

Eyes
It takes a picture of the window exactly as it is on screen and finds each word with Apple's on-device text recognition. macOS Accessibility tells it which parts are buttons and menus.
Brain
Claude Sonnet writes the reading plan for each app and site, and turns a ⌫ into a rule. Claude Haiku explains notes in about ten seconds, with Sonnet as the backup.
Voice
ElevenLabs speaks the text and sends back when each letter is said. That timing is what moves the yellow box from word to word.
A small island
Shows what it is doing: reading, thinking, paused. Pause, speed and Explained live there too.
External monitors
The box and the island follow the window to whichever screen it is on, ultrawide included.
Plays nice with WhisperTool
When you start dictating with my other app, ReadTool goes quiet.
